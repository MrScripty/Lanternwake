narrator: You carry the sealed reel to the archive because the correct machine is there and because the keeper's kitchen has held enough for one day. narrator: The reel begins with an identification spoken by an inquiry clerk. It is a deposition made three months after the flood. Ivo's voice sounds thinner than it does on the house recordings. A chair creaks before almost every answer. You hear him arranging his body around the act of speaking.
clerk: At what point did you understand that the west crew had not received a valid evacuation instruction?
ivo: At twenty-one oh-nine, when the harbor repeated the east route and nobody repeated the west correction.
clerk: Did you call the harbor on the dedicated instruction channel?
ivo: Not then.
clerk: Why not?
ivo: I had sent the correction on the measurement channel. During the trial, the routing was meant to pass an urgent flag across. I thought it had crossed. Then I knew it had not. I kept trying to restore that route instead of using the direct line.
narrator: Tomas stops the tape only long enough to mark a damaged section. Nobody speaks while he adjusts the head alignment. The pause feels like a door held open in a room you do not want to enter.
clerk: Was the routing issue known before the trial acceptance?
ivo: Yes.
clerk: Why did you sign the system ready for supervised use?
ivo: Because I believed I could supervise the weakness. Because the forecast marks had been right. Because I wanted the instrument to be useful before somebody took it away. Those are reasons. They are not a defense.

narrator: You have heard the sentence before, about channels. This time it means something larger and worse. A person can understand a safeguard and still make himself the exception he expects it to tolerate.

## ch4_s2 | No Single Keeper | archive | evening | ada,tomas,nessa,sera
narrator: The second half of the reel is less dramatic and more important to anyone who will have to verify the first. The clerk asks about dates, office copies, who initialed the routing change, and which pages Ivo supplied to the summary editor. Tomas plays every answer. None of you has earned the right to call the boring part unnecessary.
sera: There are three distinct failures here. A known channel-routing problem accepted under supervision that wasn't adequate. An obsolete rescue route issued by the coordinator. A failure to make and verify a direct correction when the mismatch became clear. Then there is a fourth, separate wrong: the later public account concealed those facts.
nessa: Don't let the list make them sound independent. One put pressure on the next.
sera: Agreed. Separate for attribution, connected in cause.
ada: And the machine predicting a surge doesn't make any of those instructions inevitable.
sera: Exactly. The measurement could be accurate while the response was wrong. A forecast of water is not a command to send a boat east.
narrator: Nessa lays the modern repeat-back protocol beside the old handover. On one page, named people acknowledge specific instructions. On the other, responsibility sits in the passive voice: the warning will be relayed, the channel will be monitored, the route will be confirmed. No sentence says by whom.
nessa: This is why I am irritating about names on a list.
tomas: You've earned a little more irritation.
nessa: I'll try not to become extravagant.
narrator: Tomas writes the six crew members' names on a new source card: Ewan Rook, Leila Fen, Ruth Merrin, Oren Pike, Hester Cole, and Dev Malik. He has written them so often that the movement looks practiced. He adds their roles, then stops before adding anything sentimental.
tomas: They were a crew. They were doing maintenance and recovery work. I don't want us to turn them into six symbols because that's easier than six people.
ada: Can the public archive include their ordinary records too? Photographs, interviews, what they made, if the families want that.
tomas: If the families want it. That part matters.
narrator: You draft a short catalogue note. You name the original sources, distinguish direct quotation from interpretation, and state the limits. The records establish that the public claim of a vessel hold is unsupported and contradicted by the original measurement log and testimony. They establish Ivo's knowledge and omission. They do not determine every individual legal liability, and tonight's team does not pretend to conduct a new tribunal around a tape deck.
ada: 'These materials warrant independent review and correction of the public record.'
nessa: Keep that. Remove 'finally.' It tells people how to feel about the timing instead of showing them the dates.
tomas: The dates are worse.
narrator: You remove it. Precision has become a form of solidarity rather than restraint. It prevents the work from being dismissed as grief with a microphone.

## ch4_s3 | The Public Line | harbor | dusk | ada,nessa,tomas
narrator: The harbor loudspeaker is an ugly gray box mounted too close to a drainpipe. Nessa has no intention of making it a stage. She uses it to repeat the evacuation route, the check-in location, and the weather update. Then she hands Tomas a radio with a separate local information channel already selected.
nessa: People at the hall know you will speak about the archive. Keep the safety channel clear. Say what we found and where the records will be available. We don't need the whole deposition over a speaker while people carry bags.
tomas: I wrote something short.
ada: Do you want us to hear it first?
tomas: Yes. Not to make it kinder. To make it true.
narrator: He unfolds a sheet. The first draft begins with his father. He reads two lines, stops, and turns the page over. On the back is another version.
tomas: 'Today we located original records relating to the 1998 west quay flood. They contradict the published claim that the workboat crew disobeyed a hold instruction. The surviving log identifies that phrase as a water-level measurement. Additional testimony describes failures in routing, rescue instructions, and later reporting. Copies have been lodged off island for independent review. The original records will remain available with their sources and limitations.'
nessa: That's good.
tomas: It sounds small.
ada: It's a large thing in sentences people can check.
narrator: He adds the six names. He asks people with relevant records to preserve them and contact the archive after the storm. He does not ask anyone to decide whom to forgive. When he speaks over the local information channel, his voice settles into the measured rhythm he uses to identify a reel.
tomas: Ewan Rook. Leila Fen. Ruth Merrin. Oren Pike. Hester Cole. Dev Malik. These records concern the conditions in which they worked and died. They deserve an account that can withstand being read closely.
narrator: The radio gives its small end-of-transmission click. For a moment, nothing answers. Then a woman at the school hall says the statement was received, the names were heard, and the transcript will be printed beside the source information. It is an ordinary acknowledgment. Tomas closes his eyes.
ada: What were you expecting?
tomas: I don't know. Something would change in the air, perhaps. A terrible expectation to put on air.
nessa: The record changed. Air can do its own work.

## ch4_s5 | Permission to Leave | keeper_house | dusk | ada,nessa
narrator: There is just enough time to cross the covered passage to the house and collect your bag. Nessa comes with you. She says it is because nobody should split from the group during an operation, which is true. It may not be the only true thing.
narrator: Ivo's letter lies in its sleeve beside the inventory. You read the line about leaving again. The first time, it sounded like a puzzle-maker's challenge disguised as permission. Now it sounds like a limited kindness from a man who had been generous with objects and miserly with the truth.

nessa: Convenient if it did.

nessa: Keep them. They stop a bad thing from pretending it was the only thing, and a good thing from pretending it cancels the bad.
