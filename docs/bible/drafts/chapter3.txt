# ch3 | What the Water Kept
## ch3_s1 | Beneath the Postcard | tide_cave | late afternoon | ada,sera,nessa
narrator: The path to the lower works begins behind a locked gate and follows the cliff on the landward side of a steel rail. Nessa checks the tide mark before unlocking it. Sera checks the forecast. You check the soles of the boots Nessa lent you. None of the precautions makes the descent feel adventurous, which is probably the point.
nessa: We have forty minutes in the chamber before this path becomes a bad choice. We leave earlier if the gauge reaches yellow. Nobody negotiates with yellow.
sera: I have set a timer for thirty. Ten minutes of margin is not ten minutes to spend.
ada: Understood.
narrator: The tide chamber is a natural hollow enlarged by human impatience. Rough basalt gives way to poured concrete halfway along its floor. Pipes run into the rock as if the cliff has been fitted with plumbing. Through a thick green inspection window, a column of water stands higher than the sea outside. It trembles at the top without spilling.
ada: How is it holding that height?
sera: Pressure difference. An upper reservoir, a lower sea inlet, and a narrow return. Most of what you're looking at is ordinary engineering. The part in the middle is why I'm here.
narrator: She directs a torch at a dark annulus surrounding the inspection window. It looks like glass until the light passes across it. Then layers appear inside, repeating at angles that refuse to settle into a single reflection.
sera: A naturally occurring resonant inclusion in the basalt. The surveyors named it phase glass. That is a historical label, not a claim that it is literally glass. The builders arranged the water path around it instead of breaking through it.
ada: Why?
sera: According to the proposal, it produced pressure responses before the measured input. At first they thought there was an error in the instruments. That was a sensible place to begin.
nessa: Then somebody wanted to sell early flood warnings.
sera: Yes. Another sensible ambition. It doesn't excuse the jump from an ambition to a reliable service.

narrator: Sera sets a caliper across two exposed reference bolts and compares the distance with the maintenance sheet. She reads the number twice. Nessa writes it down once, then repeats it back. The little ritual from the harbor office has followed you underground.
sera: The pressure path is coupled to the tower's chronograph. A stylus can receive motion from this loop. There is also an input platen upstairs, where a person can drive a stylus manually. The plans are specific about what travels: a trace, not an object. Not a mind. Not a spoken voice.

sera: We've got what we came to measure. Staying because we budgeted longer would be a strange kind of thrift.

## ch3_s2 | Two Kinds of Keeping | archive | early evening | ada,tomas,sera
narrator: Tomas has prepared the original reel while you were below. A printed card beside the recorder reads SOURCE, COPY, INTERPRETATION. Under source lies the reel. Under copy lies a storage drive. Under interpretation lies nothing yet. The blank space feels less alarming than the blank chronograph strip. It means someone is waiting to know enough.
tomas: Before we listen, I need to say something obvious because the tower is making obvious things feel uncertain. This tape does not come from the future. It is a physical magnetic recording made in 1998. Its manufacture, leader, and recorded time announcement all fit that. The extraordinary device is not an excuse to stop dating ordinary objects.

## ch3_s4 | Twenty-Three Hours, Seventeen Minutes | lantern_room | evening | ada,sera,tomas
narrator: In the tower, Sera has exposed the input platen without disturbing the receiving paper. It is a shallow brass board with a freely moving stylus, rather like an old drafting instrument. Its linkage runs into a sealed housing. A second dial, hidden under a service flap, marks an offset in hours and minutes.
sera: The rubbing wasn't a code. It was a setting copied from this dial: twenty-three hours, seventeen minutes. The archive plans give the same interval at this pressure configuration.
ada: An advance setting.
sera: Yes. A trace entered now should arrive at the receiving stylus twenty-three hours and seventeen minutes earlier. That is the claim. We need to distinguish reading a dial from establishing what it does.
narrator: On a fresh part of the receiver strip, above your arrival notes, lie three short strokes and a circle with one side flattened. Tomas recorded them forming yesterday at 18:35 while documenting the paper feed, before Ada reached the tower. His short video includes an independently checked clock. He brings it up beside today's live view.
tomas: I called these a calibration scribble. Excellent technical term. No assumption smuggled in there.
sera: We can reproduce them at the input, but that would demonstrate a consistent match, not unrestricted choice. We already know the earlier marks. A genuinely blind repeatable experiment would need a more careful design than we have time for tonight.
ada: So we shouldn't pretend the test proves more than it does.
sera: Exactly. We can still check the linkage, pressure response, and time relation safely.
narrator: You compare the yesterday calibration video's clock with the present one. The corresponding input time is today at 17:52. There are four minutes until the measured offset closes. Sera verifies that the input mechanism moves freely, then isolates the harmless test section from the bell release. Nessa's lock remains on the shaft gate. Nothing in the test asks anyone to enter the moving-load area.
ada: Why do you look disappointed?
sera: Because if this works, the world has become more interesting than my equipment can responsibly measure tonight. That is not entirely a pleasant professional experience.
tomas: I can offer a recorder with one unreliable stop button.
sera: A generous contribution to a revolution in physics.
narrator: At the scheduled second, Sera guides three strokes and a slightly flattened circle across the platen. The input arm moves. A pressure indicator dips and rises. The receiver does not produce a new mark in the present. The earlier photograph already contains the pattern.
